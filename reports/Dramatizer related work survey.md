# Dramatizer's novelty survives, but only as a combination

**Bottom line.** No published work found as of 27 October 2026 does what Dramatizer does end to end. Dramatizer runs a quality-diversity search over position-relative paralinguistic markup for a *frozen* zero-shot voice-cloning TTS. A guard-times-Wundt reward scores candidates, farthest-point sampling picks a diverse shortlist, a human makes the final choice, and the winner warm-starts the search for the nearest voice. Each ingredient has prior art, though, and one grey-literature source takes away the simplest headline. **LAION's MOSS-TTS "emotion evolution" pages already run a genetic algorithm over TTS prompt text and inline vocal-burst tags, with automatic emotion fitness and a WER penalty** ([LAION](https://projects.laion.ai/moss-emotion-evolution-all/); [LAION edge cases](https://projects.laion.ai/moss-emotion-edgecase-evolution/)). So "first evolutionary search over TTS markup" cannot be claimed. Two 2026 papers take two more framings. **LoopTTS (EMNLP 2026)** has an audio-LLM critic propose word-level stress and pause fixes ([arXiv 2608.28970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28970)). **Zhou, Atmaja & Sakti (Interspeech 2026)** have listeners choose among candidates in an interactive GA ([arXiv 2608.00998](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00998)). What is still unclaimed is the *combination*: quality-diversity illumination with a hedonic novelty term instead of single-target maximisation, markup-only control of an unmodified cloning model, genes anchored to relative position so they transfer across lines and voices, a diverse automatic shortlist before the human pick, and warm-starting by speaker similarity. The literature also says the current design needs changes before submission. The reported metrics must be separated from the optimised ones. SQUIM and WER should be thresholds rather than maximised terms. Novelty should be measured against an archive, not one neutral render. The Wundt curve should be asymmetric. And the paper has to beat a same-budget best-of-N baseline, which a 2026 study found to be competitive with RL ([arXiv 2608.31035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31035)).

## How to read the citation marks

Every citation table below has a **Check** column with one of three codes. **V** means the research team opened the primary record (arXiv abstract or HTML, publisher or ISCA page, official repository or project page) and the stated facts come from it. **S** means the item was seen only in search results, snippets or a secondary source, so its details (and sometimes its authors or venue) need checking against the full text. **M** means the bibliographic details are from the researchers' memory and were not re-verified this session. Venues marked "(venue unverified)" were not confirmed even when the paper itself was opened. When a note gave conflicting details, the opened source wins and the conflict is flagged. In the PLAN.md convention, everything marked S or M should carry "(verify)".

The LAION pages are the most important threat. The first page evolves "the GENERAL voice description, the SCRIPT delivery cue, the carrier sentence, the LoRA strength λ" plus sampling parameters, with population 16, 10 generations and top-6 elitism. Fitness is EmoNet target-emotion strength minus 0.25·max(0, WER−0.6) ([LAION](https://projects.laion.ai/moss-emotion-evolution-all/)). The second page adds "the inline vocal-burst tags" (for example `<screams>`, `<sobbing>`) to the genome, with an "add/remove a vocal-burst tag" mutation at 20%. It uses a multi-objective score of emotion strength, burst blend, genuineness and DSP proxies, and reports that stronger λ "over-drive[s] into gibberish" ([LAION edge cases](https://projects.laion.ai/moss-emotion-edgecase-evolution/)). These pages are grey literature: no date, no authors, no paper. They are still public prior art and must be cited. The team could not date them, so a Wayback Machine snapshot is needed before anyone argues about priority.

## Eight literatures, each owning one piece of the pipeline

### 1. Inline-tag and instruction TTS supplies the control surface but never searches it

CosyVoice 2 and 3 are Dramatizer's gene alphabet. Both use a natural-language instruction ended by `<|endofprompt|>` and inline `[laughter]`, `[breath]`, `<strong>…</strong>` and `<laughter>…</laughter>`, learned from about 1.5k and 5k hours of instructed data respectively ([CosyVoice 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10117); [CosyVoice 3](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17589)). The most useful single number for Dramatizer's reward design is CosyVoice 2's own ablation. **With instructions, MOS-I is 4.06 against 2.28 without, but CER rises from 0.97 to 1.52 and speaker similarity falls from 0.817 to 0.804** ([CosyVoice 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.10117)). A reward built only from WER and SIM guards therefore pushes the search toward *no* markup. That is the empirical reason Dramatizer needs an expressiveness term at all. Every commercial and open tag system the team checked (ElevenLabs v3, Dia, Bark, Chatterbox, OpenAudio S1) leaves tag placement to a human or an LLM. Several warn that tag effects depend on the voice and can produce artefacts. ElevenLabs says experimental tags are "less consistent across voices", and Dia advises using tags sparingly ([ElevenLabs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/best-practices/prompting/eleven-v3); [Dia](https://github.com/nari-labs/dia)). That fragility is the citable motivation for per-voice search with validity checks. A 2026 non-verbal vocalisation paper reports that systems still struggle to place a requested vocalisation at a specified position ([arXiv 2609.14231](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.14231)). Word-level control has arrived through training (WeSCon at NeurIPS 2025, and EmphTTS and WordVoice in 2026), so the field is making tags work better *inside* the model. Dramatizer's complementary angle is choosing *which tags go where* on a fixed model.

### 2. LLM-written markup is one-shot and systematically under-tags

The direct "LLM writes the markup" literature makes one prediction per line and never searches. Its strongest result helps Dramatizer. **Ould Ouali et al. (ICNLSP 2025) benchmarked seven open LLMs from 7B to 32B and found that "all models consistently under-generate tags"**, producing incomplete or invalid SSML. A fine-tuned two-stage Qwen2.5 cascade raised MOS from 3.20 to 3.87 with 18 listeners ([arXiv 2508.17494](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.17494)). Search explores tag density and placement directly, so it attacks this failure mode head on. No equivalent benchmark exists for frontier LLMs or for CosyVoice's tag set, so Dramatizer must run its own LLM baseline instead of assuming the result carries over. The pattern reviewers will expect Dramatizer to beat is MultiActor-Audiobook: GPT-4o writes one emotion, tone and pitch instruction per sentence and an instruct-TTS renders it ([arXiv 2505.13082](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.13082)). StressTransfer matters because an LLM inserts emphasis markers that **CosyVoice2** renders, which is the exact substrate ([arXiv 2510.13194](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.13194)). The older supervised prominence and phrase-break predictors are a source of informed priors for initial gene positions, not competitors.

### 3. Search over TTS inputs is either old and parametric or new and single-trajectory

Evolutionary search over speech prosody is a 20-year-old niche. In every case before LAION, though, the genome was continuous and inside the synthesiser: Fujisaki F0 parameters fitted by GA ([Sato et al. 2008](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2008/sato08_interspeech.pdf)), seven DCT coefficients of an F0 contour evolved by interactive CMA-ES ([Stan et al. 2011](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-24094-2_4)), unit-selection cost weights tuned by an *active* interactive GA that models the user's preferences to reduce fatigue ([Alías et al. 2011](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167639311000057)), and prosodic modification coefficients for voice conversion ([Sato 2005](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1568494604000675)). User fatigue is the recurring problem in this line, and Dramatizer's "auto-score, then show a diverse shortlist" is the modern answer to it. That framing should be explicit in the paper. Preferential Bayesian optimisation has moved into voice design, but only over continuous speaker latents ([Udagawa et al. 2022](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/udagawa22_interspeech.html); [Tian et al. 2024/25](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17068)). That suggests BO over a fixed-length relaxation of the genome as an ablation, not as a competitor. The 2026 closed loops are all single trajectories. LoopTTS uses WER and UTMOS gates, then an AudioLLM judge that writes structured stress and pause fixes, then a Refiner trained on 42K examples ([arXiv 2608.28970](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.28970)). "Listen, Critique, and Refine" has one RL-trained LALM draft, critique and resynthesise its own audio ([arXiv 2609.24163](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.24163)). Neither searches a population of input markups. Neither is a novelty threat, but LoopTTS is the closest "critic-guided prosody editing" work and should be contrasted directly. On the output side, **Llasa shows that verifier-scored best-of-N and PRM beam search on a frozen TTS shift outputs toward each verifier's preference** ([arXiv 2502.04128](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04128v1)). That is the paradigm Dramatizer moves from token space into prompt space, and the two can be nested.

### 4. Reward optimisation of TTS documents exactly the failures Dramatizer's guards must avoid

Reward and preference optimisation changes weights, not inputs, so it is complementary rather than competing. What it contributes is a catalogue of failures. **Seed-TTS reports that a WER reward made the model produce "slower, overly clear speech"**, while an SER reward lifted "surprise" accuracy from 0.13 to 0.82 ([arXiv 2406.02430](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.02430)). **Shin et al. find that GRPO on CER and NLL "collapses prosody into monotone, unnatural speech", and that adding SIM destabilised training**. A few hundred human preference pairs per round of iterative DPO worked better ([arXiv 2509.18531](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.18531)). CosyVoice 3's own DiffRO lowered speaker similarity, which the authors call "hacking". Its emotion variant raised "happy" accuracy from 0.64 to 0.98 but "can adversely affect pronunciation" ([arXiv 2505.17589](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17589v2)). RRPO found that the policy exploited the emotion reward model with acoustic artefacts ([arXiv 2512.04552](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.04552)). In the MCLP role-play reward paper, a single style reward reportedly produced repetitive utterance tails and CER above 50% until CER was added to the reward ([arXiv 2601.22661](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22661); hacking detail from snippet). The most important result for Dramatizer's baselines is **Park & Li (2026): predictor rewards each mostly improve their own metric, the gains transfer unevenly to human A/B listeners, and best-of-8 reranking was not clearly worse perceptually than GRPO** ([arXiv 2608.31035](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.31035)). No emotion-RL paper the team found uses a non-monotone, "optimal arousal" objective. They all maximise accuracy or intensity, which leaves Dramatizer's Wundt term unclaimed. GLM-TTS's laughter reward and EMORL-TTS's local-emphasis reward train into the model the same levers Dramatizer searches.

### 5. Quality-diversity has reached sound and prompts, but not speech

The QD stack Dramatizer plans to adopt is standard. MAP-Elites, in the correct title "Illuminating search spaces by mapping elites", and CMA-ME have both been checked against arXiv ([1504.04909](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=1504.04909); [1912.02400](https://export.arxiv.org/api/query?id_list=1912.02400)). CMA-ME and CMA-MAE assume a continuous genome, though. With discrete add/remove/modify/replace operators, plain MAP-Elites or CVT-MAP-Elites fits better, and a dense 6-D grid explodes to 10^6 cells at 10 bins per axis. The closest QD-for-audio work is Jónsson, Erdem, Fasciani & Glette. Their 2025 study gives Dramatizer its most actionable descriptor result: **expert-defined sonic descriptors bias exploration toward familiar sounds, while learned behaviour spaces (PCA or autoencoders on rich audio features, retrained periodically) gave significantly more diversity, with PCA best** ([arXiv 2512.02783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02783)). Their 2026 extension adds stepping-stone and goal-switch analysis that Dramatizer could replicate on its genome lineages ([arXiv 2606.09780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09780)). Masuda & Saito already framed synthesiser sound matching as QD in 2021 ([DAFx](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/UR_KEeXEtkRfUcNkctdbVw)). On the speech side, DPP-TTS uses quality-weighted determinantal point processes to diversify prosody at *sampling* time, which is the nearest TTS analogue of a quality-times-diversity objective ([EMNLP 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.267)). QD over prompts exists only for text LLMs and images: Diverse Prompts uses a CFG plus MAP-Elites over prompt structure ([arXiv 2504.14367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14367)), and Rainbow Teaming mixes categorical and continuous axes ([arXiv 2402.16822](https://www.arxiv-vanity.com/papers/2402.16822)). The team's sweep of the QD paper list found no title mentioning speech, voice, TTS, audio or prosody in the 2023–2025 entries ([QD papers](https://quality-diversity.github.io/papers)). That sweep is the best available support for "no QD over TTS markup", and it was incomplete.

### 6. The Wundt curve has a 25-year computational pedigree and no speech application

Dramatizer's expressiveness term descends from Berlyne's inverted-U between arousal potential and hedonic value. It was operationalised by Saunders & Gero's curious design agents. Secondary sources describe their Wundt curve as a *difference of two sigmoids*, reward minus punishment, applied to novelty from a *habituating* memory ([Wu & Miao, arXiv 1502.04780](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.04780)). Maher, Merrick & Saunders then used that curiosity value as an RL reward ([BYU PDF](https://axon.cs.byu.edu/CreativeAI/Accepted/Maher.pdf)). This has two consequences for Dramatizer. First, a symmetric Gaussian bump is a simplification of the original. Second, novelty measured against one fixed neutral render has no habituation, so all candidates deviating in the same direction look equally novel. The critiques are useful too. Barto, Mirolli & Baldassarre argue that novelty and surprise are routinely conflated ([Frontiers 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00907/text)). Dramatizer's "novelty" is really *deviation magnitude*, and a true surprise measure would need an expectation model, such as the TTS's own default prosody for the line. DeSIGN recovered a Wundt-like likability-versus-novelty curve for generated designs from human ratings ([arXiv 1804.00921](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00921)). Franceschelli & Musolesi note that explicit novelty and surprise rewards are rare in generative models ([arXiv 2104.02726](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.02726)). **No study validates an inverted-U for listener preference over prosodic deviation.** Dramatizer can test that empirically with its own rating data, which would be a contribution in its own right.

### 7. Every automatic speech metric Dramatizer uses is biased against expressive delivery

The evaluation literature makes one consistent point that goes beyond simple circularity: **the metrics are not neutral toward expressiveness**. NISQA and UTMOSv2 correlated *negatively* with human naturalness ratings of conversational speech, scoring formal-sounding speech higher ([arXiv 2603.01467](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.01467v1)). SIM against a neutral reference "conflates intended emotional variation with speaker-identity drift", which led one paper to add a same-emotion reference variant, SIM-EXP ([arXiv 2602.03420](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.03420)). SQUIM's objective head predicts PESQ, STOI and SI-SDR, which are degradation-from-clean measures, and it works only at 16 kHz ([arXiv 2304.01448](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.01448)). Breathy, whispered or shouted readings probably look like noise to it. Whisper normalisation choices change WER, and Whisper hallucinates in silence ([Whisper paper](https://cdn.openai.com/papers/whisper.pdf)). That interacts badly with long dramatic pauses. Learned judges are no better: an EMNLP 2026 study found that neither MOS predictors nor audio-LLM judges "reliably capture a breadth of linguistically structured, human-perceptible speech errors" ([arXiv 2608.09930](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09930)). EmergentTTS-Eval's authors report judge hallucinations on emotional and prosodic items ([arXiv 2505.23009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.23009)). On human testing, the standard references are clear. In more than 60% of Interspeech 2014 synthesis papers, conclusions rested on fewer than 20 listeners ([Wester et al. 2015](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2015/wester15c_interspeech.html)). Scale wording and increments shift MOS ([Kirkland et al. 2023](https://www.isca-archive.org/ssw_2023/kirkland23_ssw.html)). Cooper et al.'s 2025 guide requires reporting listener counts, samples, ratings per sample, the exact question and the test, and recommends Wilcoxon and binomial tests ([arXiv 2503.03250](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03250v2)).

### 8. Prosody transfer by latent fails; warm-starting by similarity is a known, testable idea

The negative result Dramatizer should position against is **Sigurgeirsson & King (ICASSP 2023): prosody-transfer models trained with a different but prosodically related reference perform "significantly worse", meaning they learn utterance-level representations tied to speaker and text rather than transferable prosody** ([arXiv 2303.04289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.04289)). Maurya & Sigurgeirsson add that closeness in the latent reference space is not a reliable prosodic-similarity metric across texts ([arXiv 2406.06601](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06601)). Seven years of latent transfer work fights source-speaker leakage. That includes Tacotron PT, GST, CopyCat and Daft-Exprt, whose adversarial weight trades identity preservation against transferred prosody ([Daft-Exprt](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.02271)). A symbolic, position-anchored genome cannot leak source timbre by construction. That turns the open question into one no paper has measured: **how consistently does the same markup render across voices?** Modern factorised systems such as IndexTTS2, Mega-TTS 2 and Vevo show that delivery is partly speaker-independent at the model level. Their delivery channel is always audio or latent, though. No prior symbolic system re-anchors markup onto a different sentence by relative position, and the nearest analogue is Daft-Exprt's length-normalised pitch-contour comparison. On the method side, nearest-speaker seeding is a direct instance of case-injected GAs ([Louis & McDonnell 2004](https://www.cse.unr.edu/~sushil/class/gas/papers/cigar.pdf)), meta-feature warm starts for BO ([Feurer et al. 2015](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/9354)) and WS-CMA-ES. **WS-CMA-ES reports that the warm-start gain correlates with source–target task similarity** ([Nomura et al. 2021](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AAAI/article/view/17109)). That is exactly the hypothesis Dramatizer must measure, including the negative-transfer case. Hieratron's experience is a warning: choosing a prosodically closest source speaker raised similarity by about 0.05 but cut quality by about 0.18 ([arXiv 2110.03347](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2110.03347)).

## The gap is the combination, and the claim must say so

**Precise gap.** No peer-reviewed or grey-literature work found does all of the following:

1. Treats the inline paralinguistic markup and instruction text of a *frozen, unmodified* zero-shot voice-cloning TTS as a genome.
2. Searches it with a quality-diversity method whose quality term is a set of identity, intelligibility and validity guards, and whose diversity or interest term is a hedonic (Wundt) function of prosodic behaviour.
3. Presents a diversity-selected shortlist rather than a single argmax to a human who makes the final choice.
4. Transfers the chosen delivery to new lines and new voices through position-relative genes, warm-starting each new voice from its nearest neighbour in speaker-embedding space.

Each clause has precedents: LAION for clause 1 with a GA, DPP-TTS and Jónsson et al. for clause 2's diversity, Sato and Zhou et al. for clause 3's human selection, and CIGAR and WS-CMA-ES for clause 4's seeding. None combines them, and clauses 2's Wundt term and 4's symbolic cross-voice transfer have no speech precedent at all.

**Threats, ranked.**

1. **LAION MOSS-TTS** removes "first evolutionary or genetic search over TTS prompts and inline tags" and "first automatic emotion fitness with an intelligibility guard". It does *not* cover QD, novelty, a speaker-similarity guard, a human shortlist, position genes or transfer. It also alters the model through LoRA λ, which leaves "markup-only control of a frozen cloning model" free. Its single-objective elitist GA is the natural ablation partner.
2. **Zhou et al. (Interspeech 2026)** removes "first interactive evolution for TTS", which Sato's 2002 GECCO work had already removed. Dramatizer differs because the human picks once from an automatically pre-filtered, diversity-selected archive instead of driving every generation, and the genome is symbolic.
3. **LoopTTS (EMNLP 2026)** takes "automatic critic proposes stress and pause edits", but as a single-pass correction with a trained refiner. Dramatizer's contrast is population exploration of diverse acceptable deliveries rather than repair of defects.
4. **Lesser threats.** "Listen, Critique, and Refine" and Step-Audio-EditX are learned or fixed refinement loops. Llasa is token-space search. None is a threat to the narrowed claim, but all must be cited.

**Narrowed novelty claim (suggested wording).** "To our knowledge, as of October 2026, Dramatizer is the first quality-diversity search over the inline paralinguistic markup of a frozen zero-shot voice-cloning TTS. It combines speaker-identity, intelligibility and validity guards with a hedonic (Wundt-curve) novelty objective over prosodic behaviour, offers a diversity-selected shortlist for human choice, and transfers discovered deliveries across lines and voices via position-relative genes and speaker-similarity warm-starting. Evolutionary search over TTS prompts and tags (LAION), interactive evolution for TTS (Sato; Zhou et al.) and critic-guided prosody correction (LoopTTS) are prior work that we extend." The hedge "to our knowledge" is needed because GECCO 2024–2026, EvoMUSART 2024–2026, SSW 2025 and ICASSP 2026 tables of contents were not browsed title by title, and the older part of the QD paper list was not read.

## Seven baselines and eight design changes the literature forces

**Baselines.** Run every search-based baseline at the same TTS-render budget.

1. The neutral render: no markup, best of 3, as in Dramatizer 1.
2. **Guard-only best-of-N** over random seeds with no markup. Park & Li show this is a strong perceptual baseline, so Dramatizer's claim must rest on its expressive objective, not on search alone.
3. Random markup genomes, filtered by the same guards.
4. **One-shot frontier-LLM markup** in CosyVoice syntax, plus a MultiActor-style per-line natural-language instruction, to test the under-generation finding beyond 7–32B models.
5. An **LLM critique-and-revise loop on the frozen model**: LoopTTS and AgentSteerTTS logic, but editing markup, since no published system does this.
6. A **LAION-style elitist GA** maximising a single emotion-strength or arousal score with a WER penalty, which isolates the value of QD plus Wundt over single-target evolution.
7. Dramatizer with the novelty term removed (guards only).

**Required ablations.**

- Farthest-point shortlist against top-k and against random-k.
- Nearest-voice seed against random seed, random-other-speaker seed and no seed.
- Relative-position genes against word-index genes on cross-line transfer.
- Hand-crafted 6-D descriptors against a learned PCA space.

Step-Audio-EditX is an optional system-level comparison: a different control paradigm on the same task.

**Design changes.**

1. **Separate reward from judge.** Every optimised scorer needs an architecturally different reporting counterpart. Whisper WER pairs with a different ASR family plus a silence-hallucination check. Resemblyzer SIM pairs with WavLM-large-SV (the Seed-TTS-eval protocol) and ECAPA-TDNN. SQUIM pairs with UTMOSv2 and DNSMOS, labelled "predicted MOS". The six DSP descriptors pair with emotion2vec shift, an SER arousal/valence regressor, AutoPCP and a pairwise audio-LLM judge. The descriptors themselves are reported only as manipulation checks. This fixes Dramatizer 1's known flaw ("the reward we optimise is also the number we report") ([Cooper et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03250v2); [Seed-TTS](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.02430)).
2. **SQUIM as a guard only.** It measures degradation from clean speech, not naturalness, so maximising it pushes toward neutral delivery. Use it as a threshold.
3. **WER and SIM as zones relative to the neutral render, not maximised terms.** Accept when WER is no more than the neutral WER plus δ (Park & Li's CER zone). Set the SIM threshold relative to the neutral render's SIM, and where possible add a SIM-EXP-style same-emotion reference. Seed-TTS and Shin et al. show that minimising WER flattens prosody.
4. **Replace deviation-from-neutral with archive novelty.** Use k-NN sparseness over an archive (Lehman & Stanley), MAP-Elites over a CVT archive, or Dominated Novelty Search, which needs no grid bounds. This gives the habituation the curious-agent models have and fixes Dramatizer 1's "novelty ignores earlier candidates".
5. **Learned behaviour space.** Following Jónsson et al., compare the 6-D hand-crafted descriptors with a periodically refit PCA of a richer feature set (eGeMAPS or a prosody/emotion embedding). Optionally add a categorical "dominant markup family" axis, in the style of Rainbow Teaming, so the archive also varies *how* expressiveness is achieved.
6. **Asymmetric Wundt curve.** Use Saunders' difference of sigmoids with a broad plateau. Over-deviation is already punished by the WER and SIM guards, so a symmetric Gaussian double-penalises it. Better still, move the plausible-range constraint into the archive's measure bounds and keep the objective as quality.
7. **Quality-aware shortlist.** Once an archive exists, take one elite from each of k farthest-apart occupied cells, seeded from the neutral render or the best candidate, or use a quality-weighted greedy DPP as in DPP-TTS. Plain FPS is start-point-sensitive and favours outliers ([arXiv 2411.16995](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.16995)).
8. **Richer variation and seeding.**
   - An ELM/LMX-style LLM mutation operator proposes context-appropriate tags.
   - A grammar over CosyVoice markup guarantees validity by construction, as in Diverse Prompts.
   - Text-prominence predictors give informed initial gene positions.
   - An inner best-of-N per genome, nested inside the outer search as in Llasa.
   - Warm starts from the top-k genomes of the nearest *several* voices, in the WS-CMA-ES style, instead of a single winner.
   - Human picks logged as winner-versus-shortlist preference pairs for later DPO, following UNO and Shin et al.

## Evaluating without grading your own homework

The main claim should rest on **scene-anchored pairwise preference**. Ask "which delivery better fits this line and direction?" with the text shown. Compare Dramatizer against neutral and against each baseline. Add a separate naturalness CMOS or MOS to detect any cost, and a same/different speaker-similarity rating against the reference. Use held-out speakers and scripts, randomised order, attention checks and duplicate-pair consistency checks. Recruit **well over 20 listeners**, analyse with binomial or Clopper–Pearson tests for preferences and Wilcoxon or Mann–Whitney for ordinal scores, and report the exact question, scale, listener count, samples per system and ratings per sample ([Cooper et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03250v2); [Wester et al.](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2015/wester15c_interspeech.html)). Best-worst scaling over the N candidates of one line gives the key validity statistic: **the rank correlation between Dramatizer's composite score and human preference.** If that correlation is weak, optimising the composite is not justified, whatever the search finds.

Pre-register the held-out judges, and tune guard thresholds and the Wundt peak on separate speakers and scripts from the test items. Choosing judges after seeing which one favours Dramatizer is leakage. Report diversity on a *fixed* external space: QD-score and coverage in a fixed CVT archive plus DS-WED ([arXiv 2509.19928](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19928v2)). Report it alongside WER and SIM distributions of the archive elites, to show diversity was not bought with quality. For transfer, measure how often a seeded genome survives the guards on the new voice and how close its fitness is to a from-scratch search. Plot that against speaker cosine similarity, and quantify negative transfer. Measure cross-voice markup consistency with AutoPCP, rate Spearman correlation and pause alignment from the Seamless toolkit. Run the EmergentTTS-Eval emotions and paralinguistics subsets as an external benchmark, and Seed-TTS-eval test-en to show baseline cloning WER and SIM are unharmed. Expect MOS predictors to score dramatised renders *below* neutral ones. Given the 2026 conversational-naturalness result, a predicted-MOS drop is not evidence of lower quality, and the paper should say so before a reviewer does. Finally, collect the listener-preference-versus-deviation curve. A first empirical test of the Wundt hypothesis for speech prosody makes the reward choice a finding rather than an assumption.

An ACL-family venue via ARR is plausible because LoopTTS, Beyond Naturalness and MultiActor-style work appear there, but the evolutionary angle is a weaker fit. A sensible sequence is an EvoMUSART short paper or an Interspeech full paper first, then GECCO or a journal (IEEE/ACM TASLP) with the full transfer study.

## Conclusion

The survey changes what Dramatizer's paper is about. "We evolve TTS markup" is now prior art. The defensible contributions are three claims nobody has tested. First, a hedonic novelty objective with QD finds *more distinct acceptable* deliveries than single-target evolution or best-of-N at equal budget. Second, symbolic, position-anchored deliveries transfer across voices where latent prosody transfer demonstrably does not, with transfer success predictable from speaker similarity. Third, listener preference over prosodic deviation follows an inverted U. Each claim is falsifiable with the baselines above, and each answers a specific gap: the absence of diversity objectives in TTS RL, Sigurgeirsson & King's negative transfer result, and the missing speech validation of Berlyne's curve.

The literature also suggests that Dramatizer's automatic scorers are its weakest link. Every scorer it uses is documented to favour neutral, over-articulated or formal speech. The design should treat them as constraints and let diversity plus the human pick carry the expressive judgement. Logging those human picks as preference pairs turns Dramatizer into a data engine for the DPO-on-prosody approach that Shin et al. found works where automatic rewards fail. That gives the project a natural second paper.
