Closeness ratings are judged against Dramatizer: an evolutionary search over paralinguistic markup edits in a zero-shot TTS prompt (CosyVoice 2/3), a 6-D prosody behaviour vector relative to a neutral render, reward = validity x intelligibility x same-speaker x Wundt(novelty), farthest-point shortlist, and a planned move to MAP-Elites/CMA-ME (pyribs) with a novelty archive.

## Core QD and novelty-search references (novelty search, MAP-Elites, CMA-ME/CMA-MAE, pyribs, surveys, novelty archive and k-NN)

### Takeaway
Dramatizer's current reward is close to a single-objective novelty search with a hedonic transform. The canonical QD stack it plans to adopt is MAP-Elites (archive of per-cell elites), CMA-ME/CMA-MAE (emitters that improve archive coverage and QD-score), and pyribs (the reference implementation). Novelty-archive details (k-NN sparseness, archive-add threshold) come from Lehman & Stanley. Dominated Novelty Search (2025) is a recent grid-free alternative that suits a 6-D continuous descriptor with no natural bounds.

### Cited Findings
- **Novelty search: "Abandoning Objectives: Evolution Through the Search for Novelty Alone".** Lehman & Stanley, 2011, *Evolutionary Computation* 19(2). The 2008 ALife XI paper "Exploiting Open-Endedness to Solve Problems Through the Search for Novelty" came first. It replaces the objective with novelty, measured as sparseness: the mean distance to the k nearest neighbours (k=15 in the original maze experiments) among the current population plus an archive. An individual enters the archive when its novelty passes a dynamic threshold. URL: https://doi.org/10.1162/EVCO_a_00025 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: high** for the novelty computation. Dramatizer's "distance in behaviour space" is the same idea, but it is measured relative to one neutral reference, not k-NN over an archive, and it is passed through a Wundt bump rather than maximised.
- **Novelty Search with Local Competition (NSLC): "Evolving a Diversity of Virtual Creatures through Novelty Search and Local Competition".** Lehman & Stanley, GECCO 2011. It combines novelty with fitness compared only against behavioural neighbours, and is the conceptual precursor of QD [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium.** It is a ready-made template for combining quality (intelligibility, speaker similarity) with novelty without collapsing them into one product.
- **MAP-Elites: "Illuminating search spaces by mapping elites".** Mouret & Clune, 2015, arXiv. It discretises a user-defined behaviour/feature space into a grid and keeps the highest-fitness solution per cell. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1504.04909 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. The main strength of MAP-Elites is its simplicity to understand and to implement ([Chatzilygeroudis et al. 2020](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.04322) [search]). **Closeness: high** (it is the planned upgrade). A 6-D grid explodes combinatorially (for example 10 bins per dimension gives 10^6 cells), so CVT-MAP-Elites or a lower-dimensional projection is likely needed.
- **CVT-MAP-Elites: "Using Centroidal Voronoi Tessellations to Scale Up the MAP-Elites Algorithm".** Vassiliades, Chatzilygeroudis & Mouret, IEEE TEC 2018. It uses a fixed number of Voronoi cells for higher-dimensional descriptors. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.05729 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium-high.** It is directly relevant to a 6-D descriptor and is supported in pyribs as CVTArchive.
- **CMA-ME: "Covariance Matrix Adaptation for the Rapid Illumination of Behavior Space".** Fontaine, Togelius, Nikolaidis & Hoover, GECCO 2020. It uses CMA-ES "emitters" whose ranking is based on archive improvement, not raw fitness. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.02400 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium.** It assumes a continuous genome. Dramatizer's genome is discrete markup edits, so CMA-ME applies only if edits are parameterised continuously (for example, insertion probabilities or positions decoded from a real vector). Otherwise, plain MAP-Elites with discrete mutation operators fits better.
- **CMA-MAE: "Covariance Matrix Adaptation MAP-Annealing".** Fontaine & Nikolaidis, GECCO 2023. It adds an archive learning rate (alpha) and threshold annealing to smoothly blend optimisation and exploration. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.10752 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium.** The same continuous-genome caveat as CMA-ME applies.
- **Differentiable QD (DQD) / CMA-MEGA: "Differentiable Quality Diversity".** Fontaine & Nikolaidis, NeurIPS 2021. It includes latent space illumination (LSI) of StyleGAN guided by CLIP. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2106.03894 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: low-medium.** Dramatizer's pipeline (TTS plus prosody extraction) is not differentiable with respect to markup.
- **pyribs: "pyribs: A Bare-Bones Python Library for Quality Diversity Optimization".** Tjanaka, Fontaine, Lee, Zhang, Kalkar, Nikolaidis et al., GECCO 2023. It has a modular archive / emitter / scheduler design, with GridArchive, CVTArchive and SlidingBoundariesArchive. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.00191; docs: https://pyribs.org [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: high** (planned tool). The author list and the presence of a novelty/proximity archive in the current release should be checked against pyribs docs.
- **Survey: "Quality Diversity: A New Frontier for Evolutionary Computation".** Pugh, Soros & Stanley, *Frontiers in Robotics and AI* 2016. It is often credited with introducing the term "QD" and the QD-score metric (sum of elite fitnesses over filled cells). URL: https://doi.org/10.3389/frobt.2016.00040 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium** (background and metrics).
- **Survey: "Quality and Diversity Optimization: A Unifying Modular Framework".** Cully & Demiris, IEEE TEC 2018. It frames QD as container (grid or unstructured archive) + selector + scoring (novelty, curiosity). URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.09251 (this ID appeared in search results: [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1708.09251) [search]). **Closeness: medium.** Its "unstructured archive" (distance-threshold archive) is essentially Dramatizer's planned novelty archive.
- **Survey: "Quality-Diversity Optimization: a novel branch of stochastic optimization".** Chatzilygeroudis, Cully, Vassiliades & Mouret, 2020/2021 (book chapter). In MAP-Elites, each cell stores the best solution exhibiting particular behavioural characteristics. [arXiv 2012.04322](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.04322) [search]. **Closeness: medium** (tutorial reference).
- **Dominated Novelty Search (DNS): "Dominated Novelty Search: Rethinking Local Competition in Quality-Diversity".** Bahlous-Boldi et al., arXiv 1 Feb 2025. Its score is the average distance to the k nearest *fitter* neighbours. It needs no predefined bounds, grid or fixed distance threshold, and keeps its advantage in high-dimensional and unsupervised descriptor spaces. The authors report state-of-the-art results on standard QD benchmarks. Code: github.com/adaptive-intelligent-robotics/Dominated-Novelty-Search. [arXiv 2502.00593](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.00593) [search]. **Closeness: high** as a design option. It merges "novelty archive" and "local competition" into one fitness transform, avoiding grid-bound choices for a 6-D prosody descriptor. Peer-reviewed venue not confirmed.
- **Meta-learned QD: "Discovering Quality-Diversity Algorithms via Meta-Black-Box Optimization".** arXiv 2502.02190, 2025. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.02190) [search, details not read]. **Closeness: low.**
- **"Learning in Sparse Rewards settings through Quality-Diversity algorithms".** arXiv 2203.01027. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2203.01027) [search, details not read]. **Closeness: low.**

### Inferences
- Dramatizer's novelty definition (distance from one neutral render) measures *deviation*, not novelty in the Lehman-Stanley sense (sparseness relative to everything found so far). Candidates that all deviate in the same direction (for example, all "slower + more pauses") score equally novel. Adding a k-NN archive term, or moving to MAP-Elites, fixes the resulting mode collapse.
- In QD terms, the multiplicative reward (validity x intelligibility x same-speaker) is the *quality/objective*. The Wundt-on-deviation term arguably belongs in the *measure* space as a constraint ("keep within plausible expressive range") rather than in the objective. MAP-Elites handles this naturally by bounding the archive's measure ranges.
- Grid-free options (CVT-MAP-Elites, DNS, or an unstructured archive à la Cully & Demiris) fit better than a dense 6-D grid at TTS-scale evaluation budgets (each evaluation is a TTS render plus ASR plus a speaker-verification pass).

### Gaps
- The exact pyribs author list for the GECCO 2023 paper, and whether pyribs ships a built-in novelty/proximity archive (`ProximityArchive`) in its current release, were not verified this session.
- No empirical guidance was found on QD for very small evaluation budgets (hundreds of evaluations), which is Dramatizer's likely regime.

## QD with generative models and LLMs (QDAIF, ELM, LMX, OMNI/OMNI-EPIC, Rainbow Teaming, prompt QD, latent illumination, 2025-2026)

### Takeaway
The most relevant pattern in this line of work is "LLM as variation operator + learned or AI-judged behaviour descriptors + MAP-Elites archive" (ELM, LMX, QDAIF, Rainbow Teaming). Dramatizer's markup genome could adopt it directly: an LLM proposes markup edits, and a MAP-Elites archive over prosody descriptors stores the elites. The QD-on-prompts work (Diverse Prompts 2025; Rainbow Teaming and its 2025-2026 descendants) is the closest methodological analogue, because it also evolves text inserted into a generative model's input.

### Cited Findings
- **ELM: "Evolution through Large Models".** Lehman, Gordon, Jain, Ndousse, Yeh & Stanley, 2022, arXiv. An LLM trained on code diffs acts as an intelligent mutation operator inside MAP-Elites (Sodarace robots). URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.08896 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium.** It shows LLM-driven mutation of a symbolic genome inside MAP-Elites, an analogue for LLM-proposed markup edits.
- **LMX: "Language Model Crossover: Variation through Few-Shot Prompting".** Meyerson, Nelson, Bradley, Gaier, Moradi, Hoover & Lehman, 2023, arXiv (later ACM TELO). The LLM performs crossover by few-shot prompting on parent texts. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.12170 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium.** Markup-annotated texts could be recombined this way.
- **QDAIF: "Quality-Diversity through AI Feedback".** Bradley, Dai, Teufel, Zhang, Oostermeijer, Bellagente, Clune, Stanley, Schott & Lehman, ICLR 2024. An LLM judges both quality and diversity attributes of generated creative text (for example, sentiment or genre axes) for MAP-Elites. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13032 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium-high.** It is the same "creative generation + QD archive" framing. Dramatizer uses signal-level descriptors (prosody DSP) instead of AI-feedback descriptors, though a CLAP or emotion-model judge could add AI-feedback axes (for example arousal/valence).
- **QDHF: "Quality Diversity through Human Feedback: Towards Open-Ended Diversity-Driven Optimization".** Ding, Zhang, Clune, Spector & Lehman, 2023 (NeurIPS 2023 workshop listing; later ICML 2024 per prior knowledge, unverified). It learns the diversity metric from human similarity judgements. In a latent space illumination task with Stable Diffusion (latents optimised for CLIP match), it substantially increased image diversity and was preferred in user studies. [arXiv 2310.12103](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.12103); [NeurIPS virtual page](https://neurips.cc/virtual/2023/79195) [search]. **Closeness: medium-high.** It directly addresses the weakness of hand-crafted descriptors. Dramatizer's six prosody descriptors could be complemented by a learned "perceived expressive difference" metric from listener triplet judgements.
- **Latent Space Illumination of GANs: "Illuminating Mario Scenes in the Latent Space of a Generative Adversarial Network".** Fontaine, Liu, Khalifa, Modi, Togelius, Hoover & Nikolaidis, AAAI 2021. It runs CMA-ME over GAN latents. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.05674 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium.** It is the original "QD over generative-model inputs" pattern. Dramatizer's "input" is a discrete text-markup prompt rather than a continuous latent.
- **OMNI: "Open-endedness via Models of human Notions of Interestingness".** Zhang, Lehman, Stanley & Clune, ICLR 2024. A foundation model judges which tasks are "interestingly new". URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01711 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: low-medium.** Its "model of interestingness" is the modern counterpart to a Wundt curve.
- **OMNI-EPIC.** Faldor, Zhang, Cully & Clune, 2024 (ICLR 2025 per prior knowledge). It generates environments as code with an archive of interesting tasks. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.15568 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: low.**
- **Rainbow Teaming: "Open-Ended Generation of Diverse Adversarial Prompts".** Samvelyan et al., NeurIPS 2024. It casts adversarial prompt generation as QD, using MAP-Elites with an LLM performing mutation and judging over a grid of (risk category x attack style). [arXiv 2402.16822](https://www.arxiv-vanity.com/papers/2402.16822) [search]. **Closeness: medium-high.** It is the clearest precedent for categorical (non-continuous) descriptor axes over *prompt text*. Dramatizer could add categorical axes such as "dominant markup type" (laughter / breath / emphasis / pause).
- **RainbowPlus.** arXiv 2504.15047, 2025. It extends Rainbow Teaming with a multi-element archive (several elites per cell) and a fitness function that scores multiple prompts at once. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.15047v1) [search]. **Closeness: medium.** Multi-element cells match Dramatizer's need to keep several good variants per expressive niche for a human shortlist.
- **QDRT: "Quality-Diversity Red-Teaming: Automated Generation of High-Quality and Diverse Attackers for LLMs".** arXiv 2506.07121, 2025. It keeps the most toxic prompt per behaviour cell in a MAP-Elites archive and trains attacker generators. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07121) [search]. **Closeness: low-medium.**
- **T-MAP: "Red-Teaming LLM Agents with Trajectory-aware Evolutionary Search".** arXiv 2603.22341; an ACL Anthology PDF is listed as 2026.emnlp-main.68 (venue inferred from the search listing, not opened). It applies MAP-Elites to tool-using LLM agents. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.22341); [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.emnlp-main.68.pdf) [search]. **Closeness: low.**
- **"Diverse Prompts: Illuminating the Prompt Space of Large Language Models with MAP-Elites".** Machado Santos, da Silva Julia & Zanchetta do Nascimento, IEEE CEC 2025. A context-free grammar plus MAP-Elites over prompt structure, with descriptors such as number of examples and reasoning depth. Evaluated on seven BigBench Lite tasks. [arXiv 2504.14367](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.14367) [fetched]. **Closeness: high (methodological).** A grammar-constrained genome of prompt edits with structural descriptors is the nearest analogue to a markup-edit genome. A CFG over CosyVoice markup tokens would guarantee "validity" by construction.
- **"Large Language Models as In-context AI Generators for Quality-Diversity".** Lim, Flageat & Cully, 2024 (ALIFE 2024). The LLM generates new solutions in-context from many archive elites. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15794 [prior knowledge, not re-verified; arXiv ID uncertain]. **Closeness: medium.**
- **MEliTA: "MAP-Elites with Transverse Assessment for Multimodal Problems in Creative Domains".** Zammit, Liapis & Yannakakis, EvoMUSART 2024. It uses deep models to assess cross-modal coherence (text-image). [review page](https://www.themoonlight.io/de/review/map-elites-with-transverse-assessment-for-multimodal-problems-in-creative-domains) [search; primary URL not opened]. **Closeness: low-medium.** Its multimodal coherence check is analogous to Dramatizer's intelligibility (text-audio) gate.
- **AC/DC: "Discovering Novel LLM Experts via Task-Capability Coevolution".** arXiv 2604.14969, 2026. It uses DNS (distance to fitter neighbours in skill-vector space) for model selection. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.14969) [search]. **Closeness: low.** It shows DNS adopted in 2026 generative-AI work.
- **NoveltyBench (2025).** It evaluates LLMs for human-like output diversity and finds state-of-the-art models generate significantly less diversity than human writers. [arXiv 2504.05228](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.05228v4) [search]. **Closeness: low.** It motivates explicit diversity search on top of generative models.

### Inferences
- An LLM-as-mutation-operator (ELM/LMX style) proposing context-appropriate markup (for example, [laughter] after a joke line, [breath] before a long clause) would likely beat random insertion. The QD literature shows such operators improve archive quality, but no TTS-specific evidence exists.
- Rainbow Teaming's mix of categorical and continuous descriptor axes suggests combining Dramatizer's continuous prosody axes with a categorical "markup family" axis. The archive would then guarantee variety in *how* expressiveness was achieved, not just in the acoustic outcome.

### Gaps
- No QD work applied to audio-generative models' text prompts (TTS, text-to-music, text-to-audio) was found. All prompt-QD work targets text LLMs or image models.
- Exact venues for QDHF (ICML 2024?) and OMNI-EPIC (ICLR 2025?) are from prior knowledge and were not verified.

## QD and novelty search applied to audio, music, sound synthesis and speech

### Takeaway
There is a small but real QD-for-sound literature: Masuda & Saito (DAFx 2021) on synthesizer sound matching, and Jónsson, Erdem, Fasciani & Glette's "Sound Innovation Engine" series (2024-2026) using MAP-Elites with YAMNet-based and learned (PCA/autoencoder) behaviour spaces. On the speech side, the nearest works are DPP-TTS (quality-weighted DPP sampling of prosody), a prosody-diversity metric for zero-shot TTS (2025), and LAION's non-peer-reviewed evolutionary search over TTS emotion prompts. **No QD, MAP-Elites or novelty-search work on TTS or expressive speech synthesis was found.**

### Cited Findings
- **"Quality Diversity for Synthesizer Sound Matching".** Naotake Masuda & Daisuke Saito, DAFx 2021 (DAFx20in21, virtual Vienna). A novelty-based objective in a GA keeps the population diverse in observable audio characteristics and improves matching. The authors also frame sound matching as QD: find many parameter sets that match the target while differing in specified audio features, to assist users during sound design. [DAFx archive](https://dafx.de/paper-archive/details/UR_KEeXEtkRfUcNkctdbVw) [fetched]; PDF likely [DAFx20in21 paper 46](https://dafx2020.mdw.ac.at/proceedings/papers/DAFx20in21_paper_46.pdf) [search; not opened, so the match to this paper is unconfirmed]. **Closeness: high conceptually.** It uses QD over a synthesis-parameter genome with audio-feature descriptors and a quality term. It differs in being synthesizer parameters with a target-match fitness, not TTS markup with a hedonic-novelty fitness.
- **"Towards Sound Innovation Engines Using Pattern-Producing Networks and Audio Graphs".** Jónsson, Erdem, Fasciani & Glette, 2024. It combines CPPN + DSP audio graphs, MAP-Elites, and the pretrained YAMNet classifier defining the measurement space (Innovation Engine style). CPPN+DSP gave more diverse elites than CPPN-only. Venue: likely EvoMUSART 2024 (not confirmed in results). [paper PDF](https://uio.no/ritmo/english/people/phd-fellows/bthj/publications/sie1/sound_innovation_engine_v1.pdf); [RITMO slides](https://uio.no/ritmo/english/people/phd-fellows/bthj/Presentation/slides/Sound%20Innovation%20Engine%201.0%20(RITMO).pdf); [supporting data](https://b2find.eudat.eu/dataset/04da6905-30ba-5dd7-bed2-bc43136b6de0) [search]. **Closeness: high.** It is the closest QD-for-audio system: MAP-Elites with a pretrained audio model defining niches. It differs in generating sounds from scratch (no speech, no speaker identity) and in classifier-class niches rather than prosody axes.
- **"Exploring Definitions of Quality and Diversity in Sonic Measurement Spaces".** Jónsson, Erdem, Fasciani & Glette, arXiv 2 Dec 2025 (cs.SD/cs.NE; no peer-reviewed venue stated). MAP-Elites over synthesis parameter spaces with millions of configurations. Expert-defined sonic descriptors are argued to bias exploration toward familiar sounds. Learned behaviour spaces (PCA and autoencoders on high-dimensional audio features, retrained at regular intervals so the grid is dynamically reconfigured) achieved significantly greater diversity than handcrafted ones, and the retraining prevented stagnation. PCA performed best. [arXiv 2512.02783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02783) [fetched]. **Closeness: high for descriptor design.** It tests exactly Dramatizer's choice: hand-crafted 6-D prosody descriptors versus learned ones. Its result suggests adding (or comparing against) a PCA projection of a richer prosody/audio-embedding feature set.
- **"Quality-Diversity Search in Sound Generation: Investigating Innovation Engines for Audio Exploration".** Same authors, arXiv 8 June 2026 (cs.SD), an extended version of the 2024 paper. It adds multiple specialised CPPNs per frequency range (simpler networks with comparable performance), a behaviour space extended with sound duration (specialisation in temporal niches), and analysis of goal switches showing "stepping stones". Results are presented via an online explorer and a composition app. The abstract does not state QD-score or coverage metrics. [arXiv 2606.09780](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.09780) [fetched]. **Closeness: high.** It is the most recent (2026) QD-audio work; its "goal-switch / stepping-stone" analysis could be replicated on Dramatizer lineages.
- **Jónsson RITMO pages.** The stated motivation is automated discovery of sounds "you wouldn't know to ask for". [RITMO profile](https://www.uio.no/ritmo/english/people/phd-fellows/bthj/index.html) [search]. **Closeness: medium** (shared motivation with Dramatizer's "surprising-but-plausible readings").
- **HarmonyMapper.** CEUR-WS Vol-3626. MAP-Elites + Neo-Riemannian theory to generate diverse chord sequences without training data. [CEUR PDF](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3626/paper2.pdf) [search; authors/year not confirmed]. **Closeness: low** (symbolic music).
- **Gram-Elites.** Biemer et al., FDG/PCG workshop 2021. N-gram operators inside QD for procedural content generation. [PDF](https://pcgworkshop.com/archive/biemer2021gram.pdf) [search]. **Closeness: low-medium.** N-gram-constrained mutation is an analogue for keeping markup edits plausible.
- **Innovation Engines.** Nguyen, Yosinski & Clune, GECCO 2015: "Innovation Engines: Automated Creativity and Improved Stochastic Optimization via Deep Learning". MAP-Elites with a DNN classifier defining niches and confidence as quality. URL: https://doi.org/10.1145/2739480.2754703 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium.** It is the parent algorithm of the sound innovation engines.
- **DPP-TTS: "Diversifying prosodic features of speech via determinantal point processes".** Joo, Koh & Jung, EMNLP 2023. It uses a quality-weighted DPP to sample diverse prosody, since temperature-scaled sampling often lacks perceptual prosodic diversity. [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.267); [arXiv 2310.14663](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.14663v1) [search]. **Closeness: high (speech side).** Its quality x diversity trade-off over prosody is the closest TTS analogue. It differs in operating inside the model's prosody predictor at sampling time (no evolutionary search, no prompt/markup editing, no archive).
- **"Measuring Prosody Diversity in Zero-Shot TTS: A New Metric, Benchmark, and Exploration".** arXiv 2509.19928, 2025. It introduces the ProsodyEval human-rated dataset and the DS-WED diversity metric. Autoregressive zero-shot TTS is reported more prosodically diverse than flow-matching NAR, and DPO preference tuning trades diversity for intelligibility. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19928v2) [search; authors not captured]. **Closeness: high for evaluation.** It offers a validated prosody-diversity metric that could serve as an external check on Dramatizer's 6-D descriptor distances. Its diversity-versus-intelligibility finding mirrors Dramatizer's multiplicative reward tension.
- **"Phone-Level Prosody Modelling with GMM-Based MDN for Diverse and Controllable Speech Synthesis".** arXiv 2105.13086. It samples phone-level prosody from a mixture for diversity. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.13086) [search]. **Closeness: low-medium.**
- **LAION "Evolving maximum emotional expression — all 40 LoRAs" and the MOSS-TTS voice-acting project.** These are project pages; no author or date is shown. Model: `laion/moss-tts-local-transformer-4.55b-voice-acting-v2` + emotion LoRAs. A GA (population 16, 10 generations, elitism top 6) evolves the voice description, script delivery cue, carrier sentence, LoRA strength λ and sampling parameters. Fitness = mean target-emotion strength (EmoNet) over best-of-8 candidates, minus 0.25 x (WER - 0.6) when WER exceeds 0.6. **No diversity or novelty term.** A sibling page describes evolving natural-language performance instructions (about 10 mutants per generation from the previous champion) scored by EmoNet, plus a VoiceCLAP check of how well vocal bursts blend into speech. [emotion evolution](https://projects.laion.ai/moss-emotion-evolution-all/) [fetched]; [voice acting](https://projects.laion.ai/laion-moss-local-1.5-voice-acting-4.55b/voices.html) [search]. **Closeness: high, and the nearest practical precedent found.** It is evolutionary search over TTS text-side instructions with an intelligibility penalty. It differs in being single-objective (maximise one emotion), having no behaviour space, archive, novelty or Wundt term, and no speaker-similarity term. It is not peer reviewed and its scores are self-reported by automatic judges.
- **Emo-DPO.** arXiv 2409.10157, 2024. Emotional TTS via DPO rather than search. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.10157v1) [search]. **Closeness: low.**
- Search for MAP-Elites applied to speech or audio LLMs (red-teaming etc.) returned only text-LLM work ([search results incl. arXiv 2506.07121](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.07121)) [search].

### Inferences
- Dramatizer appears to be the first system combining (a) QD/novelty search, (b) a TTS genome of paralinguistic markup, and (c) speaker-identity and intelligibility constraints. The LAION project is the closest precedent but optimises a single emotion target.
- Jónsson et al. (2025) imply that the six hand-crafted prosody descriptors may bias the search toward "familiar" expressive readings. A useful ablation is hand-crafted 6-D versus PCA of a larger feature set (eGeMAPS or a speech-emotion/prosody embedding), measured with coverage and an external diversity metric such as DS-WED.

- No QD, MAP-Elites, novelty-search or Wundt-curve work on TTS, expressive speech synthesis or TTS prompt/markup editing was found after targeted searches ("quality diversity speech synthesis TTS evolutionary prosody", "MAP-Elites speech audio...", "evolutionary optimization instruction prompts expressive TTS"). This should be stated explicitly as a novelty claim, hedged as "to our knowledge".
- The venue of the 2024 Sound Innovation Engine paper (EvoMUSART 2024 assumed) and the ICCC 2024 paper 175 (PDF not machine-readable this session) were not confirmed.
- Whether the Jónsson papers report QD-score or coverage was not confirmed (not in the abstracts).

## Computational creativity: Wundt curve / hedonic function, curious agents, surprise metrics, Boden's criteria, and critiques

### Takeaway
Dramatizer's Wundt(novelty) term descends directly from Berlyne's arousal-potential theory, operationalised by Saunders & Gero's curious design agents (2001). They computed novelty from a habituating self-organising map's classification error and mapped it through a Wundt curve built as the *difference of two sigmoids* (reward minus punishment), not a Gaussian. Critiques: novelty and surprise are conflated; novelty seeking may be neither necessary nor sufficient for creativity; and generative-art studies (DeSIGN) find that the most novel outputs are not the most liked, which supports but also complicates a fixed inverted-U.

### Cited Findings
- **Berlyne, *Aesthetics and Psychobiology* (1971).** The inverted-U relation between arousal potential (novelty, complexity, surprise) and hedonic value; Wundt (1832-1920) originated the "optimal middle" idea [prior knowledge for the book; Wundt attribution per [Barto, Mirolli & Baldassarre 2013](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00907/text) [search]]. **Closeness: high** (theoretical basis of the reward).
- **Saunders & Gero, "A curious design agent" / curious design agents.** CAAD Futures 2001 (venue per search snippet); Saunders' PhD thesis 2002 (prior knowledge). Agents cluster artworks and assess novelty via classification error, with a habituation layer computing novelty. Interest is a non-linear Wundt curve of novelty, built from the difference of two sigmoids (one rewarding low novelty, one punishing very high novelty). [Computational Curiosity book draft, arXiv 1502.04780](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.04780); [Modelling serendipity, arXiv 1411.0440](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1411.0440) [search; secondary sources]. **Closeness: high.** Dramatizer's Gaussian bump is a simplified stand-in. Saunders' version has asymmetric tails (a plateau-then-drop shape that is not symmetric around the peak) and novelty relative to a *learned, habituating memory*, not a fixed neutral reference.
- **Artificial creativity / social creativity model (Saunders & Gero 2001).** An internal preference model based on the Wundt curve defines each agent's preferred novelty range ("similar but different" is preferred) [search snippet via the same secondary sources above]. **Closeness: medium.**
- **Maher, Merrick & Saunders, "Achieving Creative Behavior Using Curious Learning Agents".** (AAAI Spring Symposium 2008, per prior knowledge; venue unverified.) It combines Stanley's habituation model for novelty with a Wundt curve for interest, and uses the curiosity value as the RL reward. [academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/54742655/Achieving_Creative_Behavior_Using_Curious_Learning_Agents); [BYU PDF](https://axon.cs.byu.edu/CreativeAI/Accepted/Maher.pdf) [search]. **Closeness: high** (Wundt-shaped novelty used as a reward signal, as in Dramatizer).
- **Wu & Miao, "Computational Curiosity (A Book Draft)".** arXiv 1502.04780, 2015. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1502.04780) [search]. **Closeness: medium** (survey).
- **Barto, Mirolli & Baldassarre, "Novelty or Surprise?"** *Frontiers in Psychology* 2013. It argues that intrinsic-motivation theories blur novelty (not previously experienced) and surprise (violated expectation), obscuring behaviour and its neural basis. It also reports a study in which moderate objective novelty produced the highest subjective novelty, which is consistent with an inverted U. [Frontiers](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00907/text); [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC3858647/) [search]. **Closeness: medium (critique).** Dramatizer's "novelty" is really *deviation magnitude*. [arXiv 1804.00921](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1804.00921) [search]. **Closeness: medium.** It is empirical support for an inverted U in generative outputs, measured with human ratings.
- **"Creative Image Generation with Diffusion Models".** arXiv 2601.22125, 2026. Per the snippet, its creativity ratings follow the expected arousal-potential curve, rising then overshooting. Typicality, quality and novelty criteria over output sets [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium.** Its typicality/novelty split maps onto Dramatizer's "plausible but different".

### Inferences
- Dramatizer's symmetric Gaussian bump makes the penalty for "too little" and "too much" deviation equal. Saunders' difference-of-sigmoids allows a broad plateau and asymmetric falloff. This matters because under-deviation (sounds neutral) and over-deviation (garbled, unintelligible) have different costs, and the latter is already penalised by the intelligibility and same-speaker gates. A one-sided or asymmetric hedonic curve may avoid double-penalising.
- Measuring novelty against a fixed neutral render means there is no habituation. Classic curious agents habituate (novelty decays as similar items accumulate). A novelty archive gives Dramatizer habituation for free.
- The critique literature suggests reporting both novelty (distance to archive) and surprise (deviation from model-expected prosody) rather than one number labelled "novelty".

### Gaps
- No original Saunders & Gero 2001 full text was retrieved. The difference-of-sigmoids formula is from secondary sources.
- No study was found that validates a Wundt curve for *speech prosody* preference (listener liking versus prosodic deviation). This is an open empirical question Dramatizer could test with listening data.
- No Wundt-curve reward applied to LLMs or TTS was found.

## Diversity-aware selection for presenting options to humans (farthest-point, DPP, MMR) and QD evaluation metrics

### Takeaway
Greedy farthest-point (max-min) sampling is the standard 2-approximation to k-center and gives maximal coverage of the descriptor space, but it is sensitive to the start point and over-selects outliers. Quality-weighted alternatives are DPPs (as in DPP-TTS) and MMR. For evaluation, QD-score (sum of elite qualities) and coverage (fraction of filled cells) are the standard metrics, plus external diversity metrics.

### Cited Findings
- FPS iteratively picks the point farthest from those already selected, approximately minimising fill distance. It is the greedy approximation to the k-center problem. It is sensitive to the starting point (deterministic starts such as nearest-to-centroid have been proposed), and starting from extreme points biases the subset toward boundaries and outliers [Curvature Informed FPS, arXiv 2411.16995](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.16995); [Test-time instance selection, arXiv 2608.14759](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.14759); [NEPTrainKit FPS docs](https://neptrainkit.readthedocs.io/en/latest/example/farthest-point-sampling.html) [search]. **Closeness: high** (it is Dramatizer's shortlist method). Dramatizer selects among candidates that already passed quality gates, which mitigates the outlier risk. Starting from the neutral render, or from the highest-reward candidate, is a principled seed.
- Gonzalez (1985), "Clustering to minimize the maximum intercluster distance", *Theoretical Computer Science*, is the origin of the greedy FPS 2-approximation for k-center [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium** (theory citation).
- **DPPs: "Determinantal Point Processes for Machine Learning".** Kulesza & Taskar, 2012, *Foundations and Trends in ML*. The quality x diversity kernel decomposition L = q_i S_ij q_j. URL: https://arxiv.org/abs/1207.6083 [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. Under a DPP, the most diverse set has the highest selection probability [search summary]. **Closeness: high.** Quality-weighted DPP MAP (greedy) is a principled replacement for FPS that also uses Dramatizer's reward. DPP-TTS ([ACL 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.267)) already applies this to prosody.
- **MMR: "The Use of MMR, Diversity-Based Reranking for Reordering Documents and Producing Summaries".** Carbonell & Goldstein, SIGIR 1998. It trades off relevance against similarity to already-selected items with a lambda parameter [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: medium.** It is a simpler quality-aware alternative to FPS.
- **Design Galleries.** Marks et al., SIGGRAPH 1997. Dispersion over output-perceptual descriptors to present diverse parameter settings to users for browsing [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. **Closeness: high conceptually.** It presents dispersed options in perceptual-feature space for human choice, essentially Dramatizer's shortlist idea for graphics/animation parameters.
- **QD metrics.** QD-score and coverage are defined and widely used in pyribs and the QD literature (Pugh et al. 2016; pyribs docs) [prior knowledge, not re-verified]. Jónsson et al. 2025 report diversity as the main comparison when descriptor spaces differ ([arXiv 2512.02783](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.02783)) [fetched]. When descriptor spaces differ, QD-score and coverage are not comparable across them, so a fixed external measurement space is needed. DS-WED ([arXiv 2509.19928](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19928v2)) is one candidate for prosody.
- Some diverse-subsampling algorithms degraded to near-random sampling in at least one evaluation, so visual or coverage checks of selections are advised [search summary of an [ar5iv paper, 2206.10812](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2206.10812)] [search; not read].

### Inferences
- Once Dramatizer has a MAP-Elites archive, the shortlist can be "one elite from each of k farthest-apart occupied cells". This yields an FPS-over-archive procedure that is quality-aware by construction.
- For reporting, use (i) QD-score and coverage in a *fixed* 6-D prosody grid (or CVT), (ii) an external prosody-diversity metric (DS-WED), and (iii) intelligibility (WER/CER) and speaker-similarity distributions of archive elites, which together show that diversity was not bought with quality.

### Gaps
- No user study was found comparing FPS versus DPP versus MMR for presenting *audio* alternatives to listeners.
- Primary sources for Gonzalez 1985, MMR 1998 and Design Galleries 1997 were not fetched this session.

## Has QD or a Wundt-curve reward ever been applied to expressive speech synthesis or TTS prompt markup?

### Takeaway
**None found.** After targeted searches, no published work applies QD (MAP-Elites, CMA-ME, novelty search) or a Wundt-curve / hedonic novelty reward to expressive speech synthesis, TTS prompts or paralinguistic markup. The nearest neighbours each cover one part of Dramatizer:
- LAION's MOSS-TTS emotion evolution is evolutionary prompt search for TTS, but single-objective, with no novelty term, no archive, and no peer review.
- DPP-TTS (EMNLP 2023) does quality-weighted diversity of prosody, but by sampling, not search.
- Jónsson et al. 2024-2026 and Masuda & Saito 2021 use MAP-Elites for non-speech sound synthesis.
- Saunders & Gero / Maher et al. use Wundt-curve novelty rewards for visual and design agents.

### Cited Findings
- No QD/evolutionary TTS-prosody work surfaced for "quality diversity speech synthesis TTS evolutionary prosody"; the results were DPP-TTS, GMM-MDN prosody and the ProsodyEval metric only ([DPP-TTS](https://aclanthology.org/2023.emnlp-main.267); [arXiv 2509.19928](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.19928v2); [arXiv 2105.13086](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.13086)) [search].
