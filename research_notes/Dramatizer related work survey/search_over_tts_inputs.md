# Search and Optimisation over TTS Inputs, Prosody and Voice Design (prior work for Dramatizer)

Scope note: Compiled 2026-10-27 from about 17 web searches and fetches. Items are cited only where a search result or fetched page confirmed them. Details marked "(unverified)" come only from search snippets and should be checked against the full text. Closeness ratings are judged against Dramatizer, which runs an evolutionary search over inline paralinguistic markup genomes (emphasis, pauses, [breath]/[laughter], rate, instructions) for a zero-shot LLM TTS (CosyVoice 2/3). It scores candidates automatically, shortlists them for a human pick, and seeds the next voice's search from the nearest previous voice by speaker embedding.

## Genetic algorithms / evolutionary computation for prosody, intonation or synthesis parameters

### Takeaway
Evolutionary search over prosody and synthesis parameters goes back to the 2000s HMM, unit-selection and STRAIGHT era. It was used to fit F0-model parameters with automatic fitness, to tune unit-selection cost weights with human-in-the-loop GAs, and to evolve DCT-coded F0 contours with CMA-ES. All of it searches continuous acoustic or prosodic parameters, never text-side markup.

### Cited Findings
- **Sato et al., "A Method for Automatically Estimating F0 Model Parameters and a Speech Re-synthesis Tool Using F0 Model and STRAIGHT", Interspeech 2008.** *Searched:* the parameters of the Fujisaki F0 generation model, optimised jointly with a GA because they depend on each other. *Scored:* automatically, by the MSE between the observed and model-generated F0 contours. Combined with STRAIGHT into a re-synthesis tool for listening tests. *Closeness: low.* It is an automatic-fitness GA, but it fits acoustic F0 parameters to a target rather than searching expressive text markup. — [ISCA Archive PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2008/sato08_interspeech.pdf); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/221482178_A_method_for_automatically_estimating_F0_model_parameters_and_a_speech_re-synthesis_tool_using_F0_model_and_STRAIGHT)
- **Stan, Pop, Cremene, Giurgiu, Pallez, "Interactive Intonation Optimisation Using CMA-ES and DCT Parameterisation of the F0 Contour for Speech Synthesis", NICSO 2011 (Nature Inspired Cooperative Strategies for Optimization, Springer SCI), pp. 57–71, DOI 10.1007/978-3-642-24094-2_4.** *Searched:* the phrase-level F0 contour, encoded as a genome of 7 DCT coefficients and evolved with CMA-ES. *Scored:* interactively, with listeners choosing preferred variants. It was tested on a Romanian parametric (HMM-type) synthesiser (detail from a search snippet, unverified). *Closeness: medium.* It shares interactive evolution of expressive intonation with Dramatizer, but its genome is a continuous F0 parameterisation rather than discrete inline markup, it has no automatic pre-scoring, and it has no cross-voice transfer. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-24094-2_4); [HAL record](https://hal.univ-cotedazur.fr/hal-01322772); [Epistemio](https://epistemio.com/p/nC5J465M)
- **Alías, Formiga, Llorà, "Efficient and reliable perceptual weight tuning for unit-selection text-to-speech synthesis based on active interactive genetic algorithms: A proof-of-concept", Speech Communication 53(5):786–800, 2011, DOI 10.1016/j.specom.2011.01.004.** *Searched:* the weights of the unit-selection target and concatenation cost function. *Scored:* human preference judgements, guided by an active interactive GA (aiGA) that builds a model of user preferences to reduce fatigue. The authors report more natural speech than earlier objective and subjective weight-tuning methods on a small corpus. Earlier versions appeared at ICSLP 2004 (with phonetic clustering) and ICASSP 2006 (Alías, Llorà, Formiga, Sastry, Goldberg, "Efficient interactive weight tuning for TTS synthesis: reducing user fatigue by improving user consistency"; title from a citing reference). A 2019 EURASIP paper on speech-to-singing unit selection still uses aiGA-tuned weights. *Closeness: medium-low.* It is human-in-the-loop GA for TTS with an explicit focus on reducing user fatigue, which matches Dramatizer's shortlist idea, but the genome is synthesiser-internal cost weights, not per-line markup. — [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0167639311000057); [Graph-theoretic measure for active iGAs, GECCO 2008 (ACM)](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1389095.1389281); [EURASIP 2019 singing paper](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s13636-019-0163-y)

### Inferences
- The older GA and ES prosody work treats the synthesiser as a parametric white box, with an F0 model or cost weights as the genome. Dramatizer's genome is instead a set of discrete, positional edits to the text prompt of a black-box neural model. That representation change appears to be new.
- The aiGA idea of modelling user preference to cut fatigue is a direct precedent for Dramatizer's "auto-score, then shortlist for the human" design.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the full text of the NICSO 2011 paper, so the population size, number of generations and listener protocol are unverified.
- I did not find GA work that optimises SSML or markup for concatenative or HMM TTS. Such work may exist in non-indexed proceedings.

## Interactive evolutionary computation (IEC) for voice, speech, timbre or sound design

### Takeaway
IEC for speech is an established niche. Yuji Sato's 2002–2005 line of work evolved prosodic modification coefficients for voice-quality conversion, and it continues into 2024–2026: real-time voice impression, and IGA-personalised emotional TTS at Interspeech 2026. In sound design, Dahlstedt's interactive patch mutation and Yee-King's SynthBot are the canonical precedents for interactive and automatic GA patch search. Takagi (2001) is the standard IEC survey.

### Cited Findings
- **Takagi, "Interactive evolutionary computation: fusion of the capabilities of EC optimization and human evaluation", Proceedings of the IEEE, 2001.** This is the standard survey framing IEC, in which human subjective evaluation serves as the fitness function. *Closeness: background only.* — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2985886_Interactive_evolutionary_computation_fusion_of_the_capabilities_of_EC_optimization_and_human_evaluation)
- **Yuji Sato, "Voice Conversion Using Interactive Evolution of Prosodic Control", GECCO 2002, pp. 1204–1211. Follow-ups:** "Achieving Shorter Search Times in Voice Conversion Using Interactive Evolution" (GECCO 2004, Springer LNCS) and "Voice quality conversion using interactive evolution of prosodic control", *Applied Soft Computing* 5(2):181–192, 2005. A book chapter, "EC Application in Speech Processing – Voice Quality Conversion Using Interactive Evolution of Prosodic Control", appeared in the InTech volume *Evolutionary Computation*. *Searched:* prosodic modification coefficients for pitch, amplitude and temporal structure. *Scored:* human subjective evaluation (IEC). The work covers both recorded speech and synthetic speech generated from text. It reports that IEC converted speech more efficiently than manual adjustment of the coefficients. *Closeness: medium.* It is the closest classic IEC precedent, with an interactive GA over prosody for an impression target. It differs in using global numeric prosody coefficients on a signal, with no text markup, no automatic pre-scoring and no cross-voice seeding. — [ScienceDirect (ASC 2005)](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1568494604000675); [Springer (2004)](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-540-24855-2_150); [IntechOpen chapter](https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/10927); [PDF](https://cdn.intechopen.com/pdfs/10927.pdf)
- **"Interactive Evolutionary Computation Improving Voice Impressions with Keeping Speaker Personality for Real-Time Speech", Springer 2024 (PPSN/LNCS-type proceedings; authors not captured, likely Sato's group, unverified).** *Searched:* the parameters of a software voice filter, using a GA. *Scored:* interactively by the user, with the aim of improving voice impression while keeping speaker personality, in real time. *Closeness: medium-low.* It is a modern IEC voice study, but on signal-filter parameters. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-71115-2_24); [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1007/978-3-031-71115-2_24)
- **Zhou, Atmaja, Sakti, "Beyond One-Size-Fits-All: Personalized and Culturally Adaptive Emotional TTS via Interactive Optimization of Individual Emotion Perception Spaces", arXiv 2608.00998 (Aug 2026), accepted to Interspeech 2026.** *Searched:* each listener's individual arousal-valence perception space, using an interactive genetic algorithm. *Scored:* listener interaction (the protocol was not given in the abstract). Evaluated with Japanese, Chinese and Indonesian participants. It claims more perceptually aligned emotion than averaged A-V values. *Closeness: medium.* It is the most recent IEC-for-expressive-TTS work, but it searches a continuous emotion-conditioning space rather than inline markup. The underlying TTS model was not stated on the abstract page. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00998)
- **Dahlstedt, interactive evolution for sound generation ("Creating and exploring huge parameter spaces: Interactive evolution as a tool for sound generation", 2001) and the Patch Mutator for the Nord Modular synthesiser.** An interactive GA proposes variations of a synth patch and the user selects among them. *Closeness: low-medium.* The "breed variations, human picks" interaction matches Dramatizer, but the domain is synth timbre. (Details come from search summaries citing a thesis; the primary paper was not fetched.) — [Yee-King DPhil thesis 2011](http://www.yeeking.net/matthew_yee-king_dphil_thesis_2011.pdf); [ResearchGate: AudioServe IGA for sound design](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/266630455_AudioServe_-_Collaborative_Interactive_Genetic_Algorithm_for_Sound_Design)
- **Yee-King & Roth, "SynthBot: An unsupervised software synthesizer programmer" (ICMC 2008, venue unverified), and Yee-King, Fedden, d'Inverno, "Automatic Programming of VST Sound Synthesizers Using Deep Networks and Other Techniques", IEEE TETCI (2018).** *Searched:* VSTi synthesiser parameters, using a GA (later compared with other methods including deep nets). *Scored:* automatically, by MFCC distance to a target sound, with no user feedback. One summary says it achieved MFCC error an order of magnitude below expert users (unverified). *Closeness: low.* It is automatic black-box GA over a generator's controls, but it matches a known target, whereas Dramatizer has an open-ended expressive goal. — [Academia.edu](https://www.academia.edu/2506778/Synthbot_An_unsupervised_software_synthesizer_programmer); [TETCI PDF (Goldsmiths)](https://research.gold.ac.uk/id/eprint/22516/1/myk_lf_vsti_programming.pdf)

### Inferences
- IEC on speech has always faced user fatigue, which is why Sato's work focused on "shorter search times" and Alías et al. on "active" iGAs. Dramatizer's hybrid design, where automatic scoring prunes the population and only a diverse shortlist reaches the human, is the same mitigation done with modern automatic proxies (ASR, speaker similarity, MOS predictors).

### Gaps
- I did not identify the authors of the 2024 Springer real-time voice-impression paper.
- I did not retrieve the IGA representation details for the 2026 Zhou et al. paper.

## Human-in-the-loop / preferential Bayesian optimisation for voice or speech design

### Takeaway
Preferential BO entered voice design through Koyama's sequential line search (SLS). Udagawa et al. (Interspeech 2022) used it to let users find a target speaker in a multi-speaker TTS speaker-embedding space. Related work uses coordinate descent, human sampling chains or slider-based HITL editing over latent speaker or prosody spaces. All of it searches continuous latent embeddings, not text markup.

### Cited Findings
- **Koyama, Sato, Sakamoto, Igarashi, "Sequential Line Search for Efficient Visual Design Optimization by Crowds", ACM TOG / SIGGRAPH 2017.** This is preferential BO in which each query is a single slider (a 1-D line search) answered by crowd workers. It was applied to photo enhancement and BRDF design, and an open-source C++/Python library is available. Follow-up: "Sequential Gallery" (sequential plane search, SIGGRAPH 2020), which needs fewer queries. *Closeness: low* (visual domain). It is the method backbone of the speech works below. — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3072959.3073598); [project](https://koyama.xyz/project/sequential_line_search/); [GitHub](https://github.com/yuki-koyama/sequential-line-search); [Sequential Gallery](https://koyama.xyz/project/sequential_gallery/)
- **Udagawa, Saito, Saruwatari, "Human-in-the-loop Speaker Adaptation for DNN-based Multi-speaker TTS", Interspeech 2022, arXiv 2206.10256.** *Searched:* the speaker-embedding space of a multi-speaker DNN TTS, using SLS (preferential BO). *Scored:* the user moves a single slider and picks the point closest to the target voice, so no reference audio is needed. *Closeness: medium-low.* It is HITL BO for TTS voice, but over a continuous speaker latent for identity rather than per-line expressive markup. — [ISCA Archive](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/udagawa22_interspeech.html); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.10256.pdf)
- **Tian, Liu, Lee, "User-Driven Voice Generation and Editing through Latent Space Navigation" (earlier title "Personalized Voice Synthesis through Human-in-the-Loop Coordinate Descent"), arXiv 2408.17068, v1 Aug 2024, v5 May 2025 (work in progress).** *Searched:* a low-dimensional learned speaker-embedding space whose axes relate to perceptual attributes (pitch, vocal tension, brightness, nasality). It uses a coordinate-descent-like process that decomposes the search into 1-D optimisations along principal directions. *Scored:* the user's auditory perception of similarity to an imagined target. Evaluated with simulations and user studies. *Closeness: medium-low.* — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17068); [HTML v4](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.17068v4)
- **van Rijn et al., "VoiceMe: Personalized voice generation in TTS", Interspeech 2022, arXiv 2203.15379.** *Searched:* a SpeakerNet-based speaker latent space, by human sampling (Gibbs-sampling-with-people style; method name unverified) to find voices matching faces. *Scored:* human choices, with independent raters judging the face–voice match. Ratings plateaued at a "good match" level. Its references list BOFFIN TTS (ICASSP 2020), a Bayesian-optimisation few-shot speaker adaptation method. *Closeness: low-medium.* — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15379); [ISCA PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/vanrijn22_interspeech.pdf)
- **"Giving Robots a Voice: Human-in-the-Loop Voice Creation and open-ended Labeling", CHI 2024, arXiv 2402.05206 (van Rijn et al., authorship unverified).** HITL search over a VITS TTS voice space to create voices that suit specific robots. *Closeness: low-medium.* — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642038); [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.05206)
- **Maurya & Sigurgeirsson, "A Human-in-the-Loop Approach to Improving Cross-Text Prosody Transfer", Interspeech 2024, arXiv 2406.06601.** Users adjust salient prosody correlates so that a reference prosody fits a different target text. There is no automated search. *Scored:* listener appropriateness ratings, with HITL renditions preferred 57.8% of the time. It also finds that closeness in the latent reference space is not a reliable prosodic similarity metric across texts. *Closeness: medium.* It is HITL refinement of per-utterance expressive delivery with transfer to new text. The last finding is a caution for Dramatizer's choice of automatic scoring proxies. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06601)
- **"Random rotational embedding Bayesian optimization for human-in-the-loop personalized music generation" (2025, PMC).** It describes BO as the gold standard for HITL optimisation and applies it in a generative model's Gaussian latent space. It is a music application, not TTS. *Closeness: low.* — [PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12637961/)

### Inferences
- The BO line favours low-dimensional continuous spaces, such as 1-D slider queries over embeddings. Dramatizer's variable-length, discrete, positional markup genome is a poor fit for standard GP-based BO, which supports its choice of an evolutionary operator set (add/remove/modify/replace). BO over a fixed-length relaxation of the genome would be a natural ablation to compare against.

### Gaps
- I did not find a published "VoiceDesign with BO" for modern zero-shot LLM TTS, or preferential BO over instruct-TTS style prompts.
- BOFFIN TTS (ICASSP 2020) was only seen as a reference inside VoiceMe; it was not fetched.

## Prompt optimisation / black-box search over text prompts for TTS or audio generation; test-time search

### Takeaway
Automatic prompt optimisation, including evolutionary methods such as EvoPrompt, is mature for LLMs, and LLMs are already used to generate SSML or style tags for TTS. However, I found no published work that runs an optimisation loop over TTS style prompts or inline tags. The nearest TTS-side "search" is test-time best-of-N or beam search with verifiers (LLaSA), which samples the model's stochastic output rather than editing its input.

### Cited Findings
- **Ye et al. (authors unverified), "LLaSA: Scaling Train-Time and Inference-Time Compute for Llama-based Speech Synthesis", arXiv 2502.04128 (2025).** *Searched:* the model's own output samples and token paths, using best-of-N with an outcome reward model (ORM) and beam search with a process reward model (PRM). *Scored:* automatically by speech-understanding verifiers, for example WavLM-based speaker similarity (emotion and ASR verifiers are also likely; unverified). *Closeness: medium.* It shows that automatic verifier-scored search improves LLM TTS, which matches Dramatizer's auto-scoring stage. Dramatizer instead searches over input markup, so the search is interpretable and transfers across voices. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04128v1); [alphaXiv](https://www.alphaxiv.org/abs/2502.04128)
- **"Inference-time Scaling for Diffusion-based Audio Super-resolution", arXiv 2508.02391 (2025).** This work compares verifiers and search algorithms. It notes the risk of "verifier hacking" when speaker-similarity or CLAP scores fluctuate within narrow margins, and finds zero-order search mitigates it. *Closeness: low* (task), but it is a relevant warning for Dramatizer's automatic fitness. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.02391)
- **Koel-TTS (EMNLP 2025)** notes significant quality variation among multiple outputs sampled for the same input. This motivates selection or search over generations. — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.1076.pdf)
- **"Enhancing Expressiveness in Vocal Conversational Agents through LLM-Generated SSML", FLAIRS (2025).** An LLM is prompted with the 34 Azure speaking styles and wraps responses in SSML style tags. The output was well-formed and applied tags appropriately. It is single-shot generation with no search or optimisation loop. *Closeness: medium.* The space of text-side expressive markup is the same as Dramatizer's, but it is filled by one-shot LLM generation rather than evolutionary search plus human choice. — [FLAIRS PDF](https://journals.flvc.org/FLAIRS/article/download/138814/144024/275983); [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/391794885_Enhancing_Expressiveness_in_Vocal_Conversational_Agents_through_Large_Language_Model-Generated_Speech_Synthesis_Markup_Language)
- **Industry practice on inline audio tags:** ElevenLabs documents tags such as [laughs], [whispers] and [sighs] that an LLM can emit. It also notes that explicit tags are more predictable than context alone but may be spoken aloud ("leakage"). LiveKit recommends calm baselines and pause tags. A Pipecat PR generates the tag vocabulary from the TTS service's supported expressions. All of this is manual or prompt-based authoring with no search. — [ElevenLabs best practices](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/overview/capabilities/text-to-speech/best-practices); [ElevenLabs expressive mode](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/eleven-agents/customization/voice/expressive-mode); [LiveKit blog](https://livekit.com/blog/prompting-voice-agents-to-sound-more-realistic); [Pipecat PR #6315](https://github.com/pipecat-ai/pipecat/pull/6315)
- **Tian, Xia, Hu, Zhu, Hu, Jiang, Huang, He, Xie, Xu, "Interactive TTS: Dynamic Speaking Style Adaptation for Expressive Speech Synthesis", arXiv 2609.25707 (Sep 2026).** It models contextual style decisions as "executable instructions" and trains with Iterative Rejection-Sampling Fine-Tuning and Context-Aware DPO. It is evaluated on VStyle and SpeechParaling-Bench. A search snippet says it builds on Qwen3-TTS (unverified). *Closeness: medium.* It generates style instructions per context and uses rejection sampling, a selection-by-score step, but at training time. No inference-time search over instructions was described. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25707)
- **"Emo-LiPO: Listwise Preference Optimization for Fine-Grained Emotion Intensity Control in LLM-based TTS", arXiv 2606.13006 (2026).** It notes that natural-language prompts convey emotion category reliably but often fail to produce distinct intensity levels, and fixes this with preference optimisation of model weights. *Closeness: low-medium.* It optimises weights rather than inputs, but supports the motivation for searching inputs. — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.13006)
- **General evolutionary prompt optimisation:** EvoPrompt-style mutation and crossover over a population of prompts suits discrete, non-differentiable text with LLM or rule-based fitness (described in a 2026 LinkedIn paper and a survey). These are not audio applications. — [Evolutionary Computation in the Era of LLMs: Survey (arXiv 2401.10034)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.10034); [LinkedIn self-evolving support system (arXiv 2608.10224)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.10224); [PromptEvolver (arXiv 2604.06061)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.06061)
- **Reference-prompt selection:** "Retrieval Augmented Generation in Prompt-based TTS" (Interspeech 2024) observes that prompt-based TTS picks speech prompts manually or at random, and proposes retrieval with a context-aware CLAP to choose them. This is selection rather than optimisation. — [ISCA PDF](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2024/xue24b_interspeech.pdf); [arXiv 2406.03714](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.03714)

### Inferences
- The literature splits into three groups:
  1. Authoring of tags and instructions, done by hand or by single-shot LLM generation.
  2. Output-side test-time search, such as best-of-N or beam search with verifiers.
  3. Weight-side preference optimisation, such as DPO, RSFT or LiPO.

  Dramatizer occupies an empty fourth cell: input-side, population-based search over inline markup, with verifier scoring plus a human pick.
- The warnings about verifier hacking and latent distance as an unreliable prosody metric suggest that Dramatizer should keep human selection in the loop and use diverse shortlists. Its design already does both.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that applies automatic prompt engineering (APE, OPRO, EvoPrompt, TextGrad) to instruct-TTS style prompts or text-to-audio prompts. The searches returned none, but coverage of 2025–2026 arXiv is not exhaustive.

## Latent-space search for expressive speech (GST weights, style tokens, speaker latents)

### Takeaway
The latent search I could confirm targets speaker identity: SLS over speaker embeddings, coordinate descent, human sampling, and BO-based few-shot adaptation. I found and verified no paper that searches GST or style-token weights with an optimiser in this session.

### Cited Findings
- **Speaker-latent search:** Udagawa et al. 2022 (SLS), Tian et al. 2024/25 (coordinate descent), VoiceMe 2022 (human sampling) and Giving Robots a Voice 2024 are described above. — [ISCA](https://www.isca-archive.org/interspeech_2022/udagawa22_interspeech.html); [arXiv 2408.17068](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.17068); [arXiv 2203.15379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.15379); [ACM](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642038)
- **"Mid-attribute speaker generation using optimal-transport-based interpolation of Gaussian mixture models", arXiv 2210.09916.** It generates new speakers by interpolating within a speaker latent space. This is a generative rather than search-based method. *Closeness: low.* — [arXiv PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2210.09916)
- **Maurya & Sigurgeirsson 2024** report that latent reference-space closeness poorly predicts perceived prosodic similarity across texts. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06601)

### Inferences
- Latent-space searches give smooth continuous spaces, but they are model-specific and not human-readable. Dramatizer's markup genome is human-readable and portable across voices, which is its main conceptual difference from this group.

### Gaps
- I did not verify in this session any GST-weight search or optimisation papers, nor Stanton et al.'s "Speaker Generation" (TacoSpawn). Both are known topics but were not confirmed, so they are not cited here.

## Cross-task transfer of found solutions (seeding search from a similar previous problem)

### Takeaway
I found no TTS or voice paper that seeds an evolutionary or BO search for a new voice from the solution found for the nearest previous voice in speaker-embedding space. The closest ideas are cross-text prosody transfer, done with a human in the loop, and few-shot speaker adaptation via BO.

### Cited Findings
- **Maurya & Sigurgeirsson 2024 transfer prosody to a new text with human adjustment.** This is transfer across lines, not across voices, and it does not seed a search. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.06601)
- **The 2026 Zhou et al. IGA work personalises per listener and culture.** The abstract does not say whether solutions transfer between listeners. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.00998)

### Inferences
- Within evolutionary computation this corresponds to "transfer optimisation" or "case-injected GAs", seeding initial populations from solved similar problems. I did not search that EC literature in this session. Dramatizer's speaker-embedding nearest-neighbour seeding, with position-relative genes, appears new within speech.

### Gaps
- No sources were retrieved for case-injected GA or evolutionary multitask transfer. That literature should be cited for the general idea.

## Is there any work applying evolutionary search to inline paralinguistic markup of a zero-shot LLM-based TTS?

### Takeaway
**None found.** Targeted searches combining genetic or evolutionary search, markup or tags, and CosyVoice, zero-shot or instruct TTS returned no such work up to late October 2026. The nearest neighbours are listed below, ranked by closeness.

### Cited Findings
- A targeted search for GA plus markup tags plus zero-shot TTS or CosyVoice returned only adjacent work. That work includes a controllable-TTS survey describing the shift "from tag-based control to natural-language instructions", word-level emotion control on CosyVoice2 (arXiv 2509.24629, no search), IndexTTS 2.5 and HD-PPT, with no evolutionary input search. — [EMNLP 2025 controllable TTS survey](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-main.40.pdf); [Word-Level Emotional Expression Control (arXiv 2509.24629)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.24629); [CosyVoice paper](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/382080501_CosyVoice_A_Scalable_Multilingual_Zero-shot_Text-to-speech_Synthesizer_based_on_Supervised_Semantic_Tokens)
- **Nearest neighbours by closeness:**
  - *Medium:* Sato's IEC prosody (2002–05); Stan et al. CMA-ES intonation (2011); Zhou et al. IGA emotional TTS (Interspeech 2026); LLaSA verifier-guided test-time search (2025); LLM-generated SSML (FLAIRS 2025); Interactive TTS instructions (2026); HITL cross-text prosody transfer (2024).
  - *Medium-low:* Alías et al. aiGA unit-selection weights (2011); Udagawa SLS speaker adaptation (2022); Tian et al. latent navigation (2024/25).

  Sources are given in the sections above.

### Inferences
- Dramatizer's combination of novel elements appears unpublished:
  1. The genome is discrete, position-relative inline markup for an LLM TTS.
  2. Mutation operators include duplicate skipping.
  3. Automatic scoring is followed by a diverse human shortlist.
  4. Winners are transferred across voices via speaker-embedding nearest neighbours.
- Each element has precedents: IEC prosody, fatigue-aware aiGA, verifier-scored test-time search, LLM-authored SSML and HITL prosody transfer.

### Gaps
- Coverage is limited by web search. The ISCA SSW 2025/2026 proceedings, the EvoMUSART 2024–2026 programmes and the GECCO 2025–2026 programmes were not browsed directly, and could contain a close match.
