IMPROVEMENT ANGLES
==================
Ideas that could make the Dramatizer's speech better: the voice model (CosyVoice 3), the genomes it is given,
and the judges that decide what counts as good. Each idea says what we saw, what to try, and how we would know
it worked. Ideas only: nothing here is decided. An idea that needs the GPU goes to Compute.txt once it is chosen.
Status: IDEA | CHOSEN | TRYING | DONE (date, result) | DROPPED (why)

Started 16 Nov 2026. The numbers come from:
  - the listeners' scores: 240 genome scores and 24 plain-line scores (Ash, Julius, Rama; 24 voices; all given
    on 11 Nov 2026, so BEFORE the 13-14 Nov changes: natural speed, speed kept to 0.85-1.15, hissing and
    vocalized noise switched off), on a scale of 0 to 5;
  - Milestone 2 (random-v2, 8 Nov): 720 genomes put through the automatic checks (backend/evaluation/MILESTONE_2.md);
  - the words calibration (15-16 Nov, Compute.txt item 7).
The scores are few (about 40 per feature, from 3 people), so a gap of a few tenths between two features is
not a finding on its own. Treat them as hints of where to look.


PART 1. THE PARALINGUISTIC FEATURES, IN GROUPS
----------------------------------------------
16 features are mapped as genes (backend/genome/genome_factory.py). Each gene either puts a CosyVoice token
next to a word ([breath], [laughter], ...), wraps a word (<strong>), adds a pause, or sets the speed
(backend/speech/tts_formatter.py). In groups:

  Score = the listeners' mean for genomes that have the feature (plain line: 3.25).
  Beat  = share of those genomes rated above their own plain line.

  What the groups show:
  - No group beats the plain line on average: every feature's mean is below 3.25. The best are mouth sounds
    (lipsmack), laughter while speaking, and plain [noise]; the worst are speed and the two noises already
    switched off (which the scores support).
  - Timing (group A) is the weakest group that is still switched on. Speed was rated with the old stretch
    method and a wider range; it has not been rated since the 13-14 Nov changes.
  - Within groups the features differ more than between groups: laughing_speech 2.65 against laughter 2.38,
    lipsmack 2.80 against clucking 1.91. So a group is a way to organise the features, not a verdict on them.

  Mapped in the formatter but not yet genes (CosyVoice 3 instructions and abilities, unused by the search):

  Genes per genome (scores): 1 gene 1.83 (9 scores), 2 genes 1.88 (25), 3 genes 2.68 (41),
  4 genes 2.19 (65), 5 genes 2.17 (100).


PART 2. IMPROVEMENT ANGLES
--------------------------

-- The voice model and how a feature is rendered --

1. Listen to every token on CosyVoice 3 before trusting it                                       IDEA
   Seen:  VERIFIED_FEATURES in tts_formatter.py is empty. The tokens were confirmed by listening on
          CosyVoice 2 only, and CosyVoice says some tokens were barely trained, so a token may be ignored,
          mumbled or turned into a noise.
   Try:   per feature, 10 renders on validation voices, heard by two people: was the sound there, and was
          it the right sound? Mark the ones that pass in VERIFIED_FEATURES and draw only those, or draw
          the others less often.
   Know:  each feature gets "heard as asked" in at least 8 of 10 clips, or is switched off like hissing.

2. Timed pauses instead of punctuation                                                           IDEA
   Seen:  the dramatic pause is "..." and the realization pause an em dash: hints whose length CosyVoice
          picks itself (SOFT_PAUSES). Dramatic pauses score 2.05, among the lowest still switched on.
   Try:   a pause gene with a length (pause_ms, already supported as a silence between segments), e.g.
          250 to 900 ms, mutated like the speed.
   Know:  pause genomes score at least as well as the "..." ones in a blind A/B, and the pause is where
          the gene put it.

3. Re-rate speed with the natural method                                                         IDEA
   Seen:  speed is the lowest-scoring feature still in use (1.69; beats the plain line 13 % of the time),
          but every score came before natural speed (pacing.py) and the 0.85-1.15 range.
   Try:   a small listening pile of speed-only genomes, natural and stretch at the same rates (the A/B
          renders from 13-14 Nov can be reused).
   Know:  if natural speed still scores under 2.5, narrow the range again or keep speed off by default.

4. Even out the loudness of inserted sounds                                                      IDEA
   Seen:  coughs, laughs and breaths come out at the model's own level, sometimes louder than the words.
   Try:   after rendering, measure each inserted sound against the speech around it and bring it to a set
          level below it; a short fade at its edges.
   Know:  fewer "artifact heard" answers and no worse PESQ or similarity.

5. Better reference clips                                                                         IDEA
   Seen:  CREMA-D's clips are short (3.0-4.8 s at most) and its clones less like the speaker
          (LIMITATIONS.md); 30 % of CREMA-D's rejected genomes failed on similarity, against 5 % for
          LibriTTS-R.
   Try:   join two or three clean clips of the same actor into one longer reference, or clean them first
          (Clean_Audio_Distortion.py). Only validation voices.
   Know:  CREMA-D's plain renders come closer to the reference (similarity) with no rise in WER.

6. Teach the model the sounds (longer term)                                                      IDEA
   Seen:  every inserted sound depends on how well CosyVoice 3 learnt its token.
   Try:   fine-tune on speech with marked laughter, breaths and pauses. First check which datasets have
          such marks and a licence that allows it; a fine-tuned model needs its own baseline.
   Know:  a new baseline run, then the same listening protocol as now.

-- The genomes the search makes --

7. Put genes where people would put them                                                          IDEA
   Seen:  a gene's place is a random point in the sentence, so a breath can land in the middle of a phrase
          or emphasis on "the".
   Try:   rules per group: breaths, sighs and pauses at a comma, a clause or the line's start; laughter at
          the end of a phrase; emphasis and accent on content words (nouns, verbs, adjectives), not on
          "a", "the" or "of". Random placement stays possible, but less often.
   Know:  in a blind A/B, rule-placed genomes beat randomly placed ones with the same features.

8. Fewer genes by default                                                                         IDEA
   Seen:  3 genes scored best (2.68); 4 and 5 genes about 2.2. Milestone 2: short lines fail much more
          often (42 % fine against 80 %), because one inserted sound is a large share of a short clip.
   Try:   at most 3 genes, and at most one inserted sound per 6 words or so.
   Know:  a higher share of fine genomes on short lines, and scores no lower.

9. Drop or rarely use the weakest features                                                        IDEA
   Seen:  clucking 1.91 and dramatic pause 2.05 score lowest of those switched on.
   Try:   after angle 1 (the listening check), draw features in proportion to how well they score
          (more lipsmack, laughing speech and [noise]; less clucking), instead of with equal odds.
   Know:  the next listening round's mean rises without losing variety (each feature still appears).

10. New genes: emotion, volume and stress inside a word                                           IDEA
   Seen:  CosyVoice 3 was trained on happy, sad and angry, loud and soft, and phoneme input that can move
          a word's stress (group H, I, J above). The formatter supports them; the genome does not.
   Try:   one gene per line for emotion or volume (they act on the whole line, so at most one), and a
          pronunciation gene that moves stress inside a word. Calibrate the sanity check on them first:
          an angry line may change the pace and the similarity.
   Know:  they pass the sanity check as often as the others and are rated at least as well.

11. Let the ratings guide the search                                                              IDEA
   Seen:  Milestone 2 drew genomes at random. 240 ratings now exist, and preference.py and the genome
          descriptors (genome_distance, diverse_shortlist) can learn from them.
   Try:   pick the next genomes near the well-rated ones (and away from the poorly rated ones), keeping
          a share of random ones so the search does not narrow too early.
   Know:  a guided round beats a random round of the same size in the listeners' scores.

12. Best of a few seeds                                                                           IDEA
   Seen:  the same genome sounds different with another seed; the words calibration showed how much plain
          renders already vary.
   Try:   render each fine genome with 2-3 seeds and keep the one the judges like best (words, similarity,
          PESQ). Costs 2-3 times the render time.
   Know:  higher scores at the same genome, measured blind.

-- The judges (what counts as good) --

13. Pace counts speech time only                                                                   IDEA
   Seen:  pace is the main reason a genome is rejected (125 of 237 failed on pace alone; 106 of the 153
          pace failures had no speed gene). Inserted sounds lengthen the clip, so words per second drop.
   Try:   measure pace over the speech only (leave out the inserted sounds and the added pauses). This is a
          change to the judge, so it needs its own calibration on plain pairs first, like item 7.
   Know:  the plain pairs still pass at least 95 %, and fewer genomes with sounds but no speed gene fail.

14. The words rule: compare with several plain renders                                            IDEA
   Seen:  the words limit lets a word be wrong (120 of 483 fine genomes in Milestone 2). The stricter rule
          tried on 15-16 Nov passed only 93.5 % of plain pairs, under the 95 % bar (Compute.txt item 7).
   Try:   a genome may not get more words wrong than the worst of 3-4 plain renders of its voice and line.
          The plain renders from item 7 can be reused.
   Know:  at least 95 % of plain renders pass, and genomes that break a word are stopped.

15. The start of a line                                                                           IDEA
   Seen:  13 of 176 baseline renders were misheard, almost all at the first word ("finally" heard as
          "find the").
   Try:   find out whether the model swallows the first word or Whisper mishears it (listen to them); if
          it is the model, a short silence or a breath before the first word may help.
   Know:  fewer first-word errors in the baseline.

-- The listening data the improvements rest on --

16. More and broader ratings                                                                      IDEA
   Seen:  all 240 genome scores came from 3 people on one day; no artifact answers were recorded with them;
          about 40 scores per feature.
   Try:   ask the artifact question in every pile, add listeners, and rate the new renders (natural speed,
          the switched-off features gone) so the groups above can be compared again.
   Know:  at least 100 scores per feature, with artifact answers, before any feature is dropped for good.


ORDER WE WOULD SUGGEST
  First, cheap and needed by the rest: 1 (listen to the tokens), 16 (ratings with artifact answers), 3 (speed).
  Then the search: 7 (placement), 8 (fewer genes), 2 (timed pauses).
  Then the judges: 13 (pace), 14 (words).
  Later: 10 (new genes), 11 (guided search), 12 (best of seeds), 4, 5, 15, 6.
